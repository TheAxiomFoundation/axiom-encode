Ground numbers that Hebrew statutory text writes as words. Israeli statutes name a number with a word far more often than with a digit: National Insurance Law section 68(b) sets one rate for the fourth child and another for the fifth, and section 68(c) a supplement for a parent entitled for three children or more, without printing 3, 4 or 5 anywhere, and Income Tax Ordinance section 34 grants two credit points and prints no 2 — so the CI numeric-grounding gate rejected correctly generated rules as ungrounded literals. Hebrew ordinals and cardinals now map to their values the way the English, French and Dutch words already do, allowing the one- or two-letter prefixes that bind to a Hebrew word (the definite article, the conjunction, the prepositions) with or without a maqaf, and refusing a match inside a longer Hebrew word. Eleven through nineteen are built from two words, unit then ten, and are matched as a pair before the single-word pass so that Income Tax Ordinance section 33A's "divided by twelve" grounds 12 rather than the 10 inside it.
