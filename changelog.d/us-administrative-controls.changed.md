In federal US statutes, regulations and guidance (`us/statute`, `us/regulation`,
`us/guidance`), condition sentences that only administer a program are no
longer complete-source-unit controls. The administrative action must be the
main verb of the main clause, after a modal or as an imperative: a notice (`If
an application is approved, the State agency shall provide the household with
written notice ...`), a verification step (`the State agency must verify ...`),
a Federal Register publication duty, an agency workflow step (`it must act on
the information as a reported change`, `shall follow the procedures in`),
`... are responsible for providing the information`, a definitions lead-in with
nothing after it, and the guidance-portal disclaimer. They need no principal
derived output, and they are left out of the paired positive/blocking case
report, although they still compete for case pairs. A sentence stays a control
when it states a numeric limit, a computation or a claimant-facing outcome;
when anything after its leading condition (or after a `;` in the same sentence)
states eligibility, participation, an amount, a count, an inclusion or
exclusion, an allowance or deduction, benefits, coverage, payment, a collection,
a denial, a closure, a removal, a termination, an effective date, an `until`
period or a penalty; or when a second predicate follows the administrative verb
(`... notify the household and the household's participation shall stop`).
Federal forms, publications, policies and rulemaking, manuals, state and non-US
sources are unchanged.
