Ground rate literals against the percent wording of Belgian legal French and
Dutch and of German statutes. Belgian sources (citations under be/ and
be-<region>/, read under a new be-BE profile) read "pour cent", "pourcent(s)",
"procent(en)", "procentpunt(en)", "pct." and "v. H." after a figure, across a
no-break space, and after a figure restated in parentheses ("(14,75) p.c. du
montant", "pour (75) pour cent"). German sources (de/) read "v. H." and a
no-break space before the marker. Every other source reads as on main: "28
pct." and "28 pour cent" stay unmarked under US, UK and Canadian citations,
and no parenthesis restates a figure there.

"pour cent" is a percent only where no count of a hundred can follow: before
the end of the text, ";", ")", "]", "|" or a full stop; before a soft closer
or a listed phrase ("du montant", "l'an", "par an", "pour chaque enfant",
"jusqu'à la fin", "à compter du"), unless a figure, a number word,
"quelques", "plusieurs", "demi" or an opening mark comes next; or before more
rates ("5 pour cent, 10 pour cent et 15 pour cent du montant"), when the
list's last figure carries "%", "p.c." or "procent" or a "pour cent" that
closes by these rules. So "2 pour cent kilogrammes", "2 pour cent et quelques
kilogrammes", '2 pour cent "kilogrammes"', "2 pour cent de deux cents
kilogrammes" and "2 pour cent et 3 pour cent kilogrammes" hold no rate: a
count at the end of a list makes counts of the whole list. Some real rates go
with them ("5 pour cent applicable aux revenus", "5 pour cent de 1000 euros",
"5 pour cent ou moins", "5 pour cent hors TVA", "5 pour cent selon la
catégorie"); main grounds none of them either. "pour mille" is no percent.
"pct" and "v. H." match in their own case only.

In a Belgian source, a figure restated in parentheses takes the marker after
them. A label-shaped parenthesis ("(75)") restates a rate only right after a
French, Dutch or German preposition or copula and before a word that spells a
percent out ("pour (75) pour cent", "tarief van (9) procent", "Satz von (15)
Prozent"); anywhere else it stays the masked label it is on main. Another
parenthesized figure is a rate before "%" or such a word ("(101) %"), but
before an abbreviation ("p.c.", "pct.", "v. H.") only as a fraction with a
decimal comma ("(14,75) p.c."). A source may define an abbreviation as
anything ("p.c." for "parties civiles"), and a whole number may count or cite
("(101) p.c. applicants"), so "pour (75) p.c. remboursés" and "taux de
(14.75) p.c." hold no rate; main grounds neither. A figure before the
parenthesis owns the marker, and a figure without a marker still never
grounds N/100.

Pipe tables: a Belgian or German header names a rate column only in the row
right above the delimiter row, when both have as many cells. That header row
starts the table's width, so pipe text above it no longer shifts the rate
column onto an amount. A body cell whose marker follows its figure types that
figure only, and an escaped pipe ("Type \| Category") stays inside its cell.
The width, body-cell and escaped-pipe fixes apply to every source. Rate
tables the corpus stores flattened stay ungrounded.
