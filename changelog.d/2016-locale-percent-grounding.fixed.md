Ground rate literals against the percent wording of Belgian legal French and
Dutch and of German statutes: "pour cent", "pourcent(s)", "procent(en)",
"procentpunt(en)", "pct." and "v. H." after a figure, across a no-break
space, and after a figure restated in parentheses ("(14,75) p.c. du
montant", "pour (75) p.c."). "pour cent" is a percent only before the end of
the text, a closing punctuation mark, or a run of closed-class words that
leads to a word other than a numeral ("du montant", "l'an", "par an",
"maximum", "jusqu'à la fin"), across any space or a line break. Anything
else may count a hundred of something, so it is no rate: a noun or a numeral
right after it ("pour cent kilogrammes", "pour cent mille"), a hyphen or an
opening parenthesis ("pour cent-mille", "pour cent (100) kilogrammes"), and
closed-class words before a figure ("pour cent à deux cents kilogrammes",
"pour cent et un"). Some real rates are dropped with them ("5 pour cent
applicable aux revenus", "5 pour cent de 1000 euros"); main grounds none of
them either. "pour mille" is no percent. "pct" and "v. H." match in their
own case only, so "28 PCT applications" and Dutch "v.h." stay unmarked. A
label-shaped parenthesis restates a rate only before a Belgian, Dutch or
German marker and right after a French, Dutch or German preposition, copula
or spelled-out number ("pour (75) p.c.", "vingt-huit (28) pour cent"). It
stays a masked label or note before "%" or an English marker, after any
other word ("article 5 bis (2) p.c."), after a spelled number that a
structural word names ("l'article six (12) p.c.", "Schedule Six (17)
Percent"), at the start of a clause, or after a figure, which owns the
marker ("5 (2) p.c."). A figure without a marker still never grounds N/100.
The Belgian and German words name a pipe-table rate column only in the one
row right above the table's delimiter row, when both have as many cells;
the delimiter row is found whatever whitespace surrounds it. Pipe text above
that row and a header that mismatches its delimiter type no column with
them. A body cell whose marker follows its figure ("28 pour cent", "28
percent") types that figure only and no longer gives its whole column rate
context, and an escaped pipe ("Type \| Category") stays inside its cell, so
it never moves a rate column onto an amount. Rate tables the corpus stores
flattened stay ungrounded.
