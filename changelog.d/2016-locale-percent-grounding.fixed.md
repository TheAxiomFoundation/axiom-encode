Ground rate literals against the percent wording of Belgian legal French and
Dutch and of German statutes. Belgian sources (citations under be/ and
be-<region>/, read under a new be-BE profile) read "pour cent", "pourcent(s)",
"procent(en)", "procentpunt(en)", "pct." and "v. H." after a figure, across a
no-break space, and after a figure restated in parentheses ("(14,75) p.c. du
montant", "pour (75) pour cent"). German sources (de/) read "v. H." and a
no-break space before the marker. Every other source reads as on main: "28
pct." and "28 pour cent" stay unmarked under US, UK and Canadian citations,
and no parenthesis restates a figure there.

"pour cent" marks a percent only where no count of a hundred can follow. It
marks one before the end of the text, ";", ")", "]", "|" or a full stop. It
marks one before a soft closer or a listed phrase ("du montant", "l'an", "par
an", "pour chaque enfant", "jusqu'à la fin", "à compter du"), unless a figure,
a number word, "quelques", "plusieurs", "demi" or an opening mark comes next.
It also marks one before more rates marked "pour cent", "%", "p.c." (in any
case, with or without its inner space), "procent(en)" or "procentpunt(en)"
("5 pour cent, 10 % et 15 pour cent du montant"), when the last marker closes
by these rules. Any other marker ("pct.", "v. H.", "percent", "Prozent") ends
the list. So "2 pour cent kilogrammes", "2 pour cent et quelques
kilogrammes", '2 pour cent "kilogrammes"' and "2 pour cent de deux cents
kilogrammes" hold no rate. A count at the end of a list makes counts of its
"pour cent" figures, whatever marks the last one, so "2 pour cent et 3 pour
cent kilogrammes" and "2 pour cent et 3 p.c. kilogrammes" hold none either.
Every reader applies these rules to the text before cleaning, so a count or
date that cleaning blanks ("2,5 pour cent de (10) kilogrammes", "1 à 2 pour
cent, 1.000.000 kilogrammes", "2,5 pour cent du 2026-01-01") leaves the rate
unread too. Some real rates go with them, and main grounds none of them
either: "5 pour cent applicable aux revenus", "5 pour cent de 1000 euros", "5
pour cent ou moins", "5 pour cent hors TVA", "5 pour cent selon la
catégorie", "5 pour cent et 3 p.c. de 200 euros", "5 pour cent et 3 pct. du
montant", and "5 pour cent et 3 p.c. Le taux", where the abbreviation's full
stop ends the sentence. "pour mille" marks no percent. "pct" and "v. H."
match in their own case only.

In a Belgian source, a figure restated in parentheses takes the marker after
them. A fraction with a decimal comma takes any marker after any word
("(14,75) p.c."). A whole or dotted figure, or a zero fraction ("(2,0)"),
needs a French, Dutch or German preposition or copula and one space right
before the parenthesis. After the parenthesis it needs "%" or a word that
spells a percent out ("taux de (101) % du montant"). The preposition follows a
space, an apostrophe or a mark a source types for one ("jusqu'à", "jusqu´à",
"jusqu`à"), or an opening bracket or quote, so "Item (117) Percent" and
"https://example.be/de (117) pour cent" hold no rate. A label-shaped
parenthesis ("(75)") needs a word that spells a percent out ("pour (75) pour
cent", "tarief van (9) procent", "Satz von (15) Prozent"); otherwise it stays
a masked label, as on main. The cleaner judges a label in the text before
cleaning, as the readers do, so a date after its marker ("pour cent
2026-01-01.") keeps it masked. A source may define an abbreviation as anything
("p.c." for "parties civiles"), and a whole number may count or cite ("(101)
p.c. applicants"), so "pour (75) p.c. remboursés" and "taux de (14.75) p.c."
hold no rate; main grounds neither. A figure before the parenthesis owns the
marker, and a figure without a marker still never grounds N/100.

A Belgian source's own markers, restatements and headers type only a reading
that takes the whole figure the Belgian way. "2,5 procent" states 2.5 percent
and never 25, "1,250 pour cent" 1.25 and never 1250, "1.250 pour cent" 1250
and never 1.25, and "(0,125) %" 0.125 and never 125. No part of a figure
states a rate by itself: not the "5" of "1.250,5", ",5", "2.,5", "2'5" or
"deux virgule 5", nor the "345,5" of "12 345,5". The same holds for any part
of "16 1/2", "1 / 2" or "16½" and for the "huit" of "vingt-huit". Any of the
ordinary, no-break, figure, thin and narrow no-break spaces joins a figure's
groups of three. An apostrophe joins digits as a dot does, and the gate reads
no Belgian value in a figure that has one ("1'250 pour cent" grounds no
rate). The solidus, the fraction slash, the division slash and the fullwidth
solidus join a fraction across any spaces within 64 characters, and
horizontal spaces within that reach join a mixed number. A hyphen, a dash or
a space joins the words of a spelled number. A conjunction ("et", "en",
"und") joins them only before "un", "onze", a Dutch or German ten or a
fraction ("vingt et un", "vijf en twintig"). The scale word of a percent
marker ends its number, so "deux pour cent et trois pour cent" stays a list.
A comma or a dot right before a figure parts a list only after a letter, a
percent sign or a closing bracket or quote ("1 pour cent,2 pour cent"). A
number word joins the figure after it by a hyphen, a dash, a space, a comma
or a dot ("deux 5", "deux,5", "2 virgule 5"). The word joins nothing when it
ends a percent marker ("3 pour cent,5 pour cent") or stands as an article
("un 5 pour cent"). The gate reads a figure printed in other decimal digits
as its ASCII twin, so "２,５ procent" grounds 0.025 and never 0.25. A whole
spelled number that main reads ("vingt-cinq", "nonante", "un quart") grounds
its rate. A figure that only English prints ("1,250.5", "1,250,000") keeps
its English value.

Before main's own markers, main's readings stand: "ein Satz von 1,125
Prozent" grounds 11.25, "1 / 2 %" 0.005, and the "huit" of "vingt-huit %"
0.08, as on main. No reader forces a rate onto any other reading, so the
"250" of "1,250 percent" no longer grounds 2.5. A restated figure the cleaner
keeps grounds only its Belgian reading, before main's markers too: "ein Satz
von (1.250) Prozent" grounds 12.5 and never 1.25. Since main reads "(1,250)"
only as 1250 and "1 250" as two figures, "taux de (1,250) pour cent" and
"taux de 1 250 pour cent" ground no rate. A comma and a space, a line break or
a group of the wrong length parts a figure in two, as main parts one before
"%" ("2, 5 pour cent" grounds 0.05). A range takes its marker only where its
separator alone parts the endpoints in the source: cleaning blanks the label
in "un droit de 1 (10) à 2 pour cent", which grounds 0.02 and not 0.01. The
same holds before "%" in a Belgian source, where main grounds 0.01. A year or
an article number before "à" still starts a range, as main reads one before
"%": "pour l'année 2026 à 6 pour cent du montant" grounds 20.26, and "art. 12
à 6 pour cent du montant" 0.12.

Pipe tables: a Belgian or German header names a rate column only in the row
right above the delimiter row, when both have as many cells. Such a header
types the cells below it and not the figures in its own cell, so
"Tarief (procent) 2026" grounds no 20.26, nor does a body row that repeats the
header. A header in main's words types its own cell as on main. That header
row starts the table's width, so pipe text above it no longer shifts the rate
column onto an amount. A body cell whose marker follows its figure types that
figure only, and an escaped pipe ("Type \| Category") stays inside its cell.
The width, body-cell and escaped-pipe fixes apply to every source. Rate tables
the corpus stores flattened stay ungrounded.
