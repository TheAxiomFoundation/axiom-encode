Ground rate literals against the percent wording of Belgian legal French and
Dutch and of German statutes: "pour cent", "pourcent(s)", "procent(en)",
"procentpunt(en)", "pct." and "v. H." after a figure, across a no-break
space, and after a figure restated in parentheses ("(14,75) p.c. du
montant", "pour (75) p.c."). A numeral or a unit after "pour cent", across
any space or a line break ("pour cent mille", "pour cent kilogrammes"), and
"pour mille" are no percent. "pct" and "v. H." match in their own case only,
so "28 PCT applications" and Dutch "v.h." stay unmarked. A figure before the
parenthesis, across any space or a comma, owns the marker ("5 (2) p.c.") and
keeps the parenthesis a masked note, and a label opening a line or a clause
("apply. (17) Percent") stays a label. A figure without a marker still never
grounds N/100. The Belgian and German words name a pipe-table rate column
only above the table's delimiter row, and a body cell whose marker follows
its figure ("28 pour cent", "28 percent") types that figure only and no
longer gives its whole column rate context. Rate tables the corpus stores
flattened stay ungrounded.
