Ground rate literals against the percent wording of Belgian legal French and
Dutch and of German statutes: "pour cent", "pourcent(s)", "procent(en)",
"procentpunt(en)", "pct." and "v. H." after a figure, across a no-break
space, and after a figure restated in parentheses ("(14,75) p.c. du
montant", "pour (75) p.c."). "pour cent" is a percent only before the end of
the text, a punctuation mark or a closed-class word ("du montant", "l'an",
"par an", "maximum"); before a noun or a numeral, across any space or a line
break ("pour cent kilogrammes", "pour cent hectares", "pour cent mille"), it
counts a hundred of something, and "pour mille" is no percent. "pct" and
"v. H." match in their own case only, so "28 PCT applications" and Dutch
"v.h." stay unmarked. A label-shaped parenthesis restates a rate only right
after a French, Dutch or German preposition, copula or spelled-out number and
before a marker ("pour (75) p.c.", "vingt-huit (28) pour cent"); after any
other word ("Item (17) Percent", "article 5 bis (2) p.c."), at the start of a
clause, or after a figure, which owns the marker ("5 (2) p.c."), it stays a
masked label or note. A figure without a marker still never grounds N/100.
The Belgian and German words name a pipe-table rate column only above the
table's delimiter row, which is found whatever whitespace surrounds it; a
body cell whose marker follows its figure ("28 pour cent", "28 percent")
types that figure only and no longer gives its whole column rate context;
and an escaped pipe ("Type \| Category") stays inside its cell, so it never
moves a rate column onto an amount. Rate tables the corpus stores flattened
stay ungrounded.
