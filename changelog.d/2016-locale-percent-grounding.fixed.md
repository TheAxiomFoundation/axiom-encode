Ground rate literals against the percent wording of Belgian legal French and
Dutch and of German statutes. Belgian sources (citations under be/ and
be-<region>/, read under a new be-BE profile) read "pour cent", "pourcent(s)",
"procent(en)", "procentpunt(en)", "pct." and "v. H." after a figure, across a
no-break space, and after a figure restated in parentheses ("(14,75) p.c. du
montant", "pour (75) pour cent"). German sources (de/) read "v. H." and a
no-break space before the marker. Every other source reads as on main: "28
pct." and "28 pour cent" stay unmarked under US, UK and Canadian citations,
and no parenthesis restates a figure there.

"pour cent" is a percent only where no count of a hundred can follow: before
the end of the text, ";", ")", "]", "|" or a full stop; before a soft closer
or a listed phrase ("du montant", "l'an", "par an", "pour chaque enfant",
"jusqu'à la fin", "à compter du"), unless a figure, a number word,
"quelques", "plusieurs", "demi" or an opening mark comes next; or before more
rates marked "pour cent", "%", "p.c." or "procent" ("5 pour cent, 10 % et 15
pour cent du montant"), when the last marker closes by these rules. So "2
pour cent kilogrammes", "2 pour cent et quelques kilogrammes", '2 pour cent
"kilogrammes"', "2 pour cent de deux cents kilogrammes", "2 pour cent et 3
pour cent kilogrammes" and "2 pour cent et 3 p.c. kilogrammes" hold no rate:
a count at the end of a list makes counts of its "pour cent" figures,
whatever marks the last one. Some real rates go with them ("5 pour cent
applicable aux revenus", "5 pour cent de 1000 euros", "5 pour cent ou
moins", "5 pour cent hors TVA", "5 pour cent selon la catégorie", "5 pour
cent et 3 p.c. de 200 euros", and "5 pour cent et 3 p.c. Le taux", where the
abbreviation's full stop ends the sentence); main grounds none of them
either. "pour mille" is no percent. "pct" and "v. H." match in their own
case only.

In a Belgian source, a figure restated in parentheses takes the marker after
them. A fraction with a decimal comma takes any marker after any word
("(14,75) p.c."). A whole or dotted figure needs a French, Dutch or German
preposition or copula and one space right before the parenthesis, and "%" or
a word that spells a percent out after it ("taux de (101) % du montant"), so
"Item (117) Percent" holds no rate. A label-shaped parenthesis ("(75)") needs
a word that spells a percent out ("pour (75) pour cent", "tarief van (9)
procent", "Satz von (15) Prozent"); otherwise it stays a masked label, as on
main. The cleaner judges a label in the text before cleaning, as the readers
do, so a date after its marker ("pour cent 2026-01-01.") keeps it masked. A
source may define an abbreviation as anything ("p.c." for "parties
civiles"), and a whole number may count or cite ("(101) p.c. applicants"),
so "pour (75) p.c. remboursés" and "taux de (14.75) p.c." hold no rate; main
grounds neither. A figure before the parenthesis owns the marker, and a
figure without a marker still never grounds N/100.

Pipe tables: a Belgian or German header names a rate column only in the row
right above the delimiter row, when both have as many cells. That header row
starts the table's width, so pipe text above it no longer shifts the rate
column onto an amount. A body cell whose marker follows its figure types that
figure only, and an escaped pipe ("Type \| Category") stays inside its cell.
The width, body-cell and escaped-pipe fixes apply to every source. Rate
tables the corpus stores flattened stay ungrounded.
