In US agency manuals, read a slash or a spaced dash between two words
(`case/individual`, `SNAP/Cash`, `Processing - SNAP`) as prose, not as division
or subtraction, so it no longer creates a complete-source-unit formula-output
obligation. Text that states an equation (`=` or `÷` beside a word or number)
keeps the generic reading, so formulas such as `Family member allowance =
(monthly maintenance needs standard - family member income) ÷ 3` stay
computations. Across the US manual corpus, computation clauses fall from
168,095 to 60,713 and none are added; on the Oregon/Utah SNAP dispatch pilot,
computation demands fall from 1,338 to 628. Statutes, regulations and non-US
sources keep the generic reading.
