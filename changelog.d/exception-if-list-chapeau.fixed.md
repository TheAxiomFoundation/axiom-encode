Pair a list chapeau such as "if they are:" with the items that follow it. A case pair now satisfies the chapeau when the toggling rule cites an excerpt from one of those items, in the chapeau's own unit, that names the toggled input. The items are branches inside the chapeau's own paragraph, so when a unit repeats a label, as with two paragraphs labeled (a), the list doesn't run on into the later paragraph and an excerpt from that paragraph's items doesn't count. The match follows the word rules that unless lists already use. Before, the complete-source check couldn't pair a chapeau that states its exclusions only in its items. The item's own wording decides whether the exception needs the input true or false, so the item "Not subject to withholding" needs `subject_to_withholding` false. An item whose wording leaves that open counts as if the rule didn't cite it: one whose wording through the excerpt holds "never", "neither", "nor", "except", "other than", "unless", "cannot", "none", "nothing", "nobody" or "nowhere", one whose wording holds two negations, such as "Not without withholding" or "Not disqualified from receiving benefits", since the check doesn't combine them, one whose excerpt stops before the end of a negation later in its clause, as "Paid when withholding" does in "Paid when withholding is absent" and "Paid to a person whose consent" does in "Paid to a person whose consent was never given", one where the name's words read with mixed polarity or don't appear, one with a negation cue the reader knows before the name's words that the reader doesn't read as negating them, because it stands too far back or a comma, "when" or "but" comes between, as in "Not, in the agency's view, subject to withholding", and one whose excerpt follows a lead-in with a negation in the same item, such as "(1) Not received from any of the following:" or "(1) Ineligible for any of the following:". The negations these checks know include the shared reader's own cues, German ones among them, the words it reads as negative in an input's name, such as "missing" and "absent", and forms it reads past, such as "lacks" and "lacked". A clause ends at a ";", ":", "and", "or" or "but", or at a "." that ends a sentence, one before an upper-case letter or the text's end that no abbreviation such as "U.S.C.", "e.g.", "Pub. L.", "Treas. Reg." or "art." holds, in either letter case. So the excerpt "Reported to the agency" from "Reported to the agency and never withheld" keeps its reading, and "never" still counts after the excerpt "Paid to a person whose consent" in "Paid to a person whose consent under 5 U.S.C. 552a was never given". The items run through the first that ends a sentence, so a labeled paragraph after a list whose last item ends a sentence doesn't count. A labeled paragraph after an item that ends with a list separator such as "; and" joins the list. An item that ends with neither a sentence end nor a list separator such as "; or" leaves the list with no items. Unlabeled text after the last item still counts as part of that item. The check reads a clause as a list chapeau only when its subject is a pronoun, or a determiner and one word. That word can't be or hold "unless" or "except", so "if the except are:" still reads as it did. A clause with more than one word after the determiner, such as "if the disabled member's payments are:", still needs a pair on that condition. So does a clause that states a condition of its own, such as "if the household so elects, the deductions are:".
